For a hyperlinked PDF planner, the best place to sell digital planners is Etsy if you want built-in buyer traffic, Gumroad or Payhip if you want to keep more margin and full hyperlink functionality, and Shopify once you have an audience. Amazon KDP is the one to avoid: it flattens navigation. The sections below run the fee math on a $12 sale and rank each platform by file support.

Do digital planners sell well enough to justify a paid marketplace?

Yes, if you treat it as a niche product with a defined buyer: demand is real but concentrated, so marketplace fees only make sense where those buyers actually browse.

What buyers actually search for: device-specific planners

Nobody searches "planner." They search "reMarkable planner 2025," "Supernote A5X template," or "Kindle Scribe digital planner." That specificity is your advantage: a hyperlinked PDF built for a 10.3" BOOX Note Air 3 screen at its native PPI beats a one-size-fits-all file every time, and buyers know it.

Why hyperlinked e-ink planners command a niche premium

Tapping an index link to jump between months, trackers, and dot grid pages is the core feature buyers pay for, and it only works if the file keeps its links intact after export and upload. Etsy's fee stack (listing, transaction, payment processing, offsite ads) takes a real cut, so price accordingly — the Etsy seller fee schedule breaks down each one.

If you design templates yourself, tools like Notebook Studio handle native-resolution export and vector PDF output, and we cover workflow questions on our blog.

What does Etsy actually take from a $12 digital planner sale?

On a $12 hyperlinked PDF planner, Etsy's fee stack is three parts: a listing fee, a transaction fee, and payment processing. Using current published rates as an illustrative example, that $12 sale costs roughly $1.59 in fees, or about 13% of the sale price, before any Offsite Ads exposure.

Here is the itemized breakdown. The listing fee is $0.20 per listing, charged whether or not the item sells, and it renews every four months or on each sale. The transaction fee is 6.5% of the item price, which is $0.78 on a $12 planner. Payment processing in the US runs about 3% plus $0.25, adding roughly $0.61. Total: about $1.59 on a $12 sale, leaving you around $10.41 before tax handling and any ad spend. If Etsy serves your listing through Offsite Ads, that cut jumps to 15% of the order, which on a $12 planner wipes out most of your margin.

The traffic advantage is why sellers pay it anyway. Etsy has people typing "digital planner reMarkable" and "hyperlinked PDF" into search every day, so a well-tagged listing for a dot grid habit tracker layout sells without you building an audience first. Pair your listing with a freebie or preview built in a dedicated PDF designer, like the templates behind https://notebook-studio.com/blog/habit-tracker-planner-layouts-e-ink, and the built-in searchers do the marketing for you.

How to sell digital planners on Amazon (KDP) — and why hyperlinked PDFs don't fit

Amazon KDP is built for print books and reflowable ebooks, not interactive PDFs. Its supported uploads are print-interior files, EPUB, and DOC/DOCX; PDF is only accepted as a print manuscript, and Kindle conversions strip the hyperlink navigation your planner depends on. Amazon's own KDP content guidelines and supported file types spell out these formats, and none of them preserve a hyperlinked PDF as a working, page-turning planner.

That means a KDP listing forces you to flatten your design: no tap-to-jump index, no linked weekly-to-daily navigation. On a reMarkable or Supernote, that kills the core feature buyers pay for.

A flattened planner still makes sense if you want print-on-demand paper planners shipped to buyers, or a discovery channel for people who'll later buy your full hyperlinked version elsewhere. Design the interactive original at native device resolutions in a dedicated PDF designer like Notebook Studio's reMarkable planner designer, then export a flattened copy for KDP. Treat Amazon as a funnel, not your main store.

Gumroad, Payhip, and Shopify: full PDF delivery, no review gate

All three hand your file to the buyer untouched, so a 400-page hyperlinked planner for a Supernote A5 X2 Manta or BOOX Go 10.3 behaves exactly as designed. There is no approval step, so you can push a corrected link map the same day. Shopify needs an app for digital delivery, which adds setup work but keeps full control.

Creative Market and Etsy: what their upload pipelines allow

Etsy and Creative Market both deliver the original file, so interactivity survives. Etsy caps file sizes per listing, which can force split zips for large planners. Creative Market runs a review before listing goes live, adding days to launch. Test your exported file on-device first; our troubleshooting page covers common link failures.

Etsy vs Gumroad vs Payhip: where should a first-time planner seller list?

Fee math on the same $12 sale across all three

On Etsy, a $12 hyperlinked PDF sale loses roughly $0.20 listing fee plus about 6.5% transaction fee and payment processing, so you keep around $10.50 before any offsite ad charges. Gumroad currently takes a flat 10% per sale plus processing, per its fee schedule, leaving you closer to $10.40. Payhip's free tier takes 5% plus processing, keeping you nearer $11, with no listing costs on any of the three.

Traffic you bring vs traffic the platform brings

Etsy is the only one that brings buyers searching for digital planners; Gumroad and Payhip expect you to drive traffic from r/Supernote, r/Onyx_Boox, or e-ink Discords. If you have no audience, Etsy's built-in search usually wins despite the fees. If you already post device-specific templates, Payhip's lower cut wins. Design your files first: Notebook Studio exports vector hyperlinked PDFs at native e-ink resolutions, though it does not list to any marketplace for you, so uploading is still manual.

When is it worth moving from a marketplace to your own Shopify store?

Monthly cost vs commission crossover

Marketplaces take a cut of every sale but charge nothing up front. Shopify charges a flat amount monthly plus a small transaction cut, so the math is simple: divide the monthly cost by the commission you currently pay per planner, and that number is your break-even point. If you sell a handful of hyperlinked PDFs a month, stay on Etsy, Gumroad, or Payhip. If you're shipping dozens, the flat rate wins and your margin per sale jumps.

Keeping the customer relationship and device-specific bundles

Your own store lets you bundle by device: one listing for the reMarkable Paper Pro in its native resolution, another for the Supernote A5 X, a third for Kindle Scribe, all sold from one product page. You also keep buyer emails, so a dot grid update or a new page-turning layout reaches past customers directly. The trade-off: you drive your own traffic, and there's no marketplace discovery doing it for you.

What file formats and templates do buyers expect on each platform?

Marketplace buyers on Etsy expect a ZIP of hyperlinked PDFs, one file per device, with a readme naming which file fits which screen. Direct-sale buyers on Payhip and Gumroad expect the same, plus a portrait and landscape variant per device. Payhip's own pricing page confirms it takes a flat 5% per sale with no listing cost, so there is no penalty for shipping a larger multi-file bundle (Payhip pricing).

Native-resolution PDFs for reMarkable, Supernote, BOOX, and Scribe

Match page dimensions to the panel, not to A4. A reMarkable 2 wants 1404 × 1872 at 226 PPI, a Kindle Scribe 1920 × 2560 at 300 PPI, and BOOX colour models need their own canvas or the dot grid drifts out of calibration. Build index tabs as link rectangles on every page so page turning works from anywhere in the planner.

Which platform should you pick, by seller profile?

First-time seller with no audience

Start on Etsy. You get buyer traffic you have not earned yet, and hyperlinked PDFs download exactly as you export them. Accept the fee stack on a $12 planner as the cost of discovery, and raise prices once reviews accumulate. Keep your files vector so page turning and internal links survive on a reMarkable or Supernote.

Established creator with an e-ink following

Move to Payhip or Gumroad once your subreddit, Discord, or mailing list drives sales. The same $12 planner keeps nearly all its margin, you control versioning, and buyers get the full hyperlinked PDF without a marketplace flattening anything. FAQ

Is selling digital planners on Etsy still profitable in 2025 after fee increases?

Yes, if you price for the fee stack. A $12 sale costs roughly $1.59 in listing, transaction, and processing fees, about 13%, before Offsite Ads. That still leaves around $10.41 per planner, and Etsy is the only channel where buyers search for device-specific planners without you building an audience first. Raise prices once reviews accumulate.

Can I sell the same digital planner on Etsy and Gumroad at the same time?

Yes. Neither platform claims exclusivity over digital files, so listing the identical hyperlinked PDF on both is standard practice. Many sellers use Etsy for discovery and Gumroad for repeat buyers who want lower-friction re-downloads or updates. Keep versioning consistent: when you fix a link map, upload the corrected file everywhere and tell past buyers.

Do I need to register a business or pay taxes to sell digital planners online?

Requirements vary by country and state, so check your local rules before your first sale. Most marketplaces and payment processors report income above certain thresholds, and Etsy collects sales tax on transactions for you in many jurisdictions. A sole proprietorship is usually enough to start; an accountant can confirm what applies to your situation.

How much should I charge for a hyperlinked digital planner for reMarkable or Supernote?

Device-specific planners command a premium because buyers search for exact models like "Supernote A5X template." Bundling multiple devices or adding portrait and landscape variants on direct-sale platforms justifies a higher price point than a single-file listing.
