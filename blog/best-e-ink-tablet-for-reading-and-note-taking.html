Best E Ink Tablet for Reading and Note-Taking (2025)

October 31, 2026 • 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • The reMarkable Paper Pro is the best pick for annotating dense academic PDFs thanks to its large 11.8-inch canvas.
  • The Supernote A5 X2 Manta suits meeting notes, with on-device handwriting-to-text and a ceramic nib that never wears down.
  • The Kindle Scribe and Kobo Elipsa 2E lead for leisure reading because of warm frontlights and bookstore-native software.
  • BOOX tablets run Android, so you can install Kindle, Kobo, and Libby apps side by side on one device.
  • Custom hyperlinked planners only feel native when built at your device's exact screen dimensions and PPI.

The best e ink tablet for reading and note-taking depends on your workflow: the Kindle Scribe suits leisure reading with light annotation, the Supernote A5 X2 Manta suits meeting notes and handwriting-to-text, and the BOOX Note Air 3 C or reMarkable Paper Pro suit heavy academic PDF annotation. This guide matches each device to a scenario, then compares pen latency and handwriting feel so you can pick by how you actually work.

Which e ink tablet is best for reading and note-taking right now?

For academic PDF annotation, pick the reMarkable Paper Pro. For meeting notes with handwriting-to-text, pick a Supernote A5 X2 Manta. For leisure reading, pick the Kindle Scribe. For a flexible do-everything device with an open app store, pick a BOOX Note Air 3. If you want the lightest writing feel, the BOOX Go 10.3 wins on paper-thin hardware. The reasoning behind each pick, including latency and handwriting feel, sits in the comparison matrix below.

The short version: screen size and PPI matter less than how the pen feels, which is why the reMarkable Paper Pro's spec sheet is only a starting point, not the verdict. If you already own one of these devices and want custom dot grid or hyperlinked notebooks tuned to its exact resolution, Marcus Reed covers that workflow in the calibration section.

Pen, latency and handwriting feel

reMarkable and Supernote ship their pens in the box; BOOX, Scribe, and Elipsa generally do not, so budget for one. Latency is lowest on reMarkable and Supernote, which is why planners built for them feel closest to paper. Supernote's ceramic nib never wears down; BOOX and reMarkable nibs need replacing.

Openness: sideloading, file formats, export

BOOX runs Android, so you can install apps. Supernote accepts EPUB and exports to common formats. reMarkable and Kobo are the most locked down, though reMarkable supports reMarkable's own file format plus PDF and EPUB viewing. If you design your own hyperlinked PDFs, all seven open them, but exact page dimensions and PPI matter for crisp rendering; we keep a per-device calibration reference on our support page.

Which tablet is best for annotating academic PDFs?

Dense two-column papers punish small screens. You want the largest canvas you can afford, because every zoom-and-pan cycle breaks the reading flow that e-ink is supposed to protect.

Why screen size dominates for academic PDFs

At 10.3 inches or larger, a full A4 page stays legible at 100% zoom, which means you can highlight a line without resizing anything. The Supernote A5 X2 Manta is a 10.3-inch option built around this use case, and its official spec page lists the resolution and PPI you would need to match a dot grid template to the panel (Supernote A5 X2 Manta specifications). On a 7-8 inch device like a base Kindle, the same paper becomes a panning exercise.

Split view matters less on e-ink than on iPad, where a PDF and a notes window can sit side by side. On most e-ink tablets, expect to flip between the paper and a linked notebook page instead.

Annotation export and search compared

Check three things before committing: whether annotations export as a flattened PDF your reference manager can index, whether handwriting search runs on-device, and whether page turning stays responsive on 300-page files. reMarkable and Supernote both handle this well; Kobo's export options are the weakest of the group.

If you build your own linked notebooks for coursework, Notebook Studio exports vector PDFs at native panel resolutions, so hyperlinks and line weight survive the transfer to any of these devices.

Which tablet is best for meeting notes and handwriting-to-text?

For capture-first work, pick a device whose handwriting conversion runs well in your language and whose notebooks you can search months later. The Supernote A5 X2 Manta and BOOX Note Air 3 both handle on-device conversion and keyword search; reMarkable's conversion happens after upload and works well for English meeting notes. The Kindle Scribe and Kobo Elipsa 2E are weaker fits here, since their software leans toward reading and markup rather than building a searchable archive of notes.

Handwriting conversion accuracy

Conversion quality depends more on your handwriting consistency than on the device. Print or lightly cursive handwriting converts far better than fast scrawl on every tablet listed above. Test with one real meeting before committing: convert a page, count the errors that matter (names, numbers), and ignore the ones that don't. BOOX devices also let you convert per-page rather than per-document, which matters when a notebook mixes notes and sketches.

Organising and retrieving notes months later

Folders beat tags on e-ink, because typing on any of these devices is slow. Name notebooks by date and project, and keep one notebook per recurring meeting. If you build your own planners or meeting templates, our guide covers sizing and linking pages so navigation works like a paper binder. Honest limitation: no e-ink tablet makes full-text search across hundreds of pages fast, so retrieval discipline matters more than the device you buy.

Which tablet is best for leisure reading?

Book-first buyers should weigh three things: frontlight quality, where you buy books, and whether you can scribble in margins without leaving the page.

Frontlight and comfort for long sessions

The Kindle Scribe and Kobo Elipsa 2E lead on reading comfort because both pair warm frontlight with bookstore-native software. The BOOX Go 10.3 skips a frontlight entirely, which matters if you read in bed or on planes. BOOX's own spec pages list the Note Air 3 C with a frontlight and the Go 10.3 without, so check the official BOOX product specifications before buying if evening reading is your main use case.

Bookstore access and library apps

Kindle and Kobo devices tie you to their stores but make borrowing simple. BOOX runs Android, so you can install Libby, Kindle, and Kobo apps side by side. If you also want a hyperlinked reading journal alongside your books, we compare our PDF designer with EP Template Studio elsewhere on this site.

What do Reddit and Discord users actually complain about?

Recurring complaints by device

On r/RemarkableTablet, the loudest gripe is the closed file system: getting documents on and off the tablet without their desktop app feels like a fight, and folder organization on-device stays shallow. Pen nib wear is the second recurring theme, with heavy writers buying replacement nibs on a schedule.

On r/Supernote, complaints skew toward template limits. The built-in page templates are fine for quick notes, but anyone building a serious digital planner hits a wall and ends up importing a hyperlinked PDF.

On r/Onyx_Boox, the pattern is firmware inconsistency: BOOX ships many models, and users report that features and stability vary between them, so the tablet you buy may behave differently after an update.

What the communities recommend instead

The common fix is DIY: import your own hyperlinked PDF planner, tuned to your device's exact PPI, so page turning and link taps land where they should. That is the gap Notebook Studio targets, with native resolutions for each device and a canvas editor for building dot grid and planner pages. Honest caveat: it designs templates, it does not fix firmware quirks or nib wear.

How should you calibrate custom notebooks and planners to your device?

Why native PPI and hyperlink navigation matter

A hyperlinked PDF only feels native if it is built at your device's exact screen dimensions and pixel count. The Kindle Scribe, for example, uses a 10.2-inch, 300 PPI display (Amazon spec page), so a template exported at the wrong resolution produces fuzzy dot grids and misaligned tap zones. Hyperlink navigation matters just as much: index pages, month tabs, and page-turn links are what separate a real digital planner from a stack of PDF pages you scroll through. Build the links once, at the correct canvas size, and every jump lands where your stylus actually is.

Designing templates without Illustrator

Notebook Studio ships with native hardware resolutions for reMarkable, Supernote, BOOX, Kindle Scribe, and Kobo devices, plus custom dimensions for anything else. You design on a studio canvas, wire up interactive hyperlink navigation, and export vector PDFs. Ep Template Studio and hyperpaper work in the same category; Canva and Adobe Express are general design tools that were not built for e-ink dimensions.

Where Notebook Studio falls short

It exports PDFs only, so you cannot edit a template after import on-device, and handwriting-to-text or cloud features are out of scope. If you want a drawing app rather than a planner builder, look elsewhere.

How to choose in five minutes: a decision path

Start with your primary workflow. Annotating academic PDFs: pick the largest canvas you can afford, since side-by-side reading and margin notes eat screen space. Meeting notes with handwriting-to-text: Supernote and BOOX both convert handwriting, so decide on stylus feel at a store if possible. Leisure reading: Kindle Scribe or Kobo Elipsa 2E, because bookstores matter more than note features.

Then apply budget, then ecosystem openness. If you sideload templates and hyperlinked notebooks, favor BOOX (Android) or Supernote (open file format) over locked devices.

Finally, ask what you will write on it. If the answer is custom dot grid planners sized to your exact PPI, you need a tool that exports vector PDFs at native hardware resolutions. Our Notebook Studio does exactly that; hyperpaper and Ep Template Studio are the alternatives to compare it against.

FAQ

Is an e ink tablet better than an iPad for note taking?

For long sessions, yes, if you value focus over features. E ink screens have no flicker or blue light, batteries last weeks, and the pen-on-paper feel reduces distraction. An iPad wins on speed, color, apps, and side-by-side reading, so the choice depends on whether you want a paper replacement or a multitool.

Can you convert handwriting to text on a reMarkable or Supernote?

Both handle it, but differently. Supernote converts on-device and supports keyword search inside notebooks. reMarkable converts after your notes upload and works well for English. On either device, consistent print or light cursive converts far better than fast scrawl, so test with a real page before judging accuracy.

Do e ink tablets work with Kindle books and library apps like Libby?

Kindle and Kobo devices are tied to their own stores but make borrowing simple. BOOX tablets run Android, so you can install the Kindle, Kobo, and Libby apps side by side on one device. reMarkable and Supernote are poor fits for bookstore reading, since their software centers on notes and PDF markup instead.

What is the best budget e ink tablet for note taking?

The BOOX Go 10.3 offers the sharpest display of the group at 300 PPI in thin hardware, though it lacks a frontlight and you must budget for a stylus. The reMarkable 2 remains a common secondhand pick. Remember that most BOOX and Kindle devices do not include a pen in the box.

Do I need a special pen, and do nibs wear out?

Each device needs its own pen family, and reMarkable and Supernote include one in the box while BOOX, Kindle Scribe, and Kobo usually do not. Nibs on BOOX and reMarkable wear with use and need replacing on a schedule for heavy writers. Supernote's ceramic nib does not wear down, which saves money long term.
